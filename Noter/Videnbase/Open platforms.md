**Open platforms** are systems that are extendable, meaning they allow third-party producers to develop and offer extensions or applications for them. In contrast, closed platforms are typically not extendable and come with pre-installed content provided solely by the platform owner.

### Importance of Open Platforms
Open platforms are significant in modern markets for several reasons:
*   **Increased Value for Consumers:** Giving consumers access to third-party extensions directly raises the platform's value. For example, high-end mobile phones (like the Nokia N95 or later iPhones) are more valuable because they allow users to install diverse software from various developers.
*   **Direct Profitability from Developers:** Platform owners can generate revenue by selling access rights to third-party developers. Conversely, they can choose to subsidize developers through training or free documentation to encourage more application development, which further enhances the platform's appeal to users.
*   **Internalizing Cross-Group Externalities:** Open platforms function as two-sided markets, connecting consumers with application providers. This allows the platform to manage the interactions between these two groups, balancing prices and fees to maximize the overall value of the ecosystem.
*   **Strategic Flexibility:** The decision to be open or closed is a key strategic choice. While opening a platform is often costless and attracts consumers, it also intensifies competition. Firms must weigh the benefits of third-party variety against the risk of more aggressive price competition for users.
